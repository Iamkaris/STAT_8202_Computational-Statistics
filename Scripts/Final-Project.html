Positive Skew: The data is heavily right-skewed (positively skewed), with a massive clump of observations near the zero-to-low dollar range and a very long tail extending toward high costs.

Frequency Breakdown: The majority of the 788 subscribers incurred relatively low costs (under \(\$5,000\)), but a small number of “high-cost” patients incurred claims exceeding \(\$20,000\), with some reaching over \(\$40,000\).

Primary Predictor (Interventions): There is a strong positive correlation (r = 0.73) between the number of interventions and the total cost. This suggests that medical procedures are the dominant factor in expense variance.

Secondary Predictors: ER Visits (r = 0.38) shows a moderate positive relationship with cost, indicating that emergency care significantly adds to the total.

Weak/Surprising Relationships: * Age (r = -0.06) has an almost negligible (and slightly negative) correlation with cost. This implies that in this specific subscriber group, age is not a primary driver of claim size.

Comorbidities (r = 0.15) and Duration (r = 0.18) show surprisingly weak linear relationships with cost, suggesting they may only be significant in combination with other factors.

Multicollinearity Warning: The correlation between Drugs and ER Visits (r = 0.53). While not high enough to be dangerous yet, it’s a signal to check the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)
